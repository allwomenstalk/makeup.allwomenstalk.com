# Frost Lipstick Is Back: How to Wear Frosted Makeup

Frost lipstick is back, and its pearly shine can be as subtle or as dramatic as you like. A blended liner gives a pale frost definition, while a deeper metallic shade can make the finish feel rich and wearable on its own.

Start with the [liner technique](#liner), explore the [shade ideas](#shades), or try a small [frosted eye accent](#eyes). There is no single shade assigned to a skin tone: undertone, contrast and the look you enjoy all matter.

## Why frost is back

Makeup artist Emily Harper tells [Cosmopolitan](https://www.cosmopolitan.com/style-beauty/beauty/a74181039/frost-lipstick-trend/) that nostalgia for Y2K makeup is part of the appeal. The magazine reports that MAC brought back Frost shades alongside new ones. It also cites Pinterest's beauty trend report: searches for “frosted makeup” rose 286%, “metallic lips” 52%, and “frosted lips 2000s” 42%. Those are Pinterest search trends, not a measure of how many people wear the look; the reported comparison period is not specified in the article.

Frost has had many lives, from pale mid-century lips to warmer and deeper metallic shades and the cool pinks associated with Y2K. Today's version can nod to any of them without recreating an entire era.

## What makes lipstick a frost?

Frost describes the reflective finish: fine pearl or metallic shimmer catches the light across the lips. The base color can be nearly sheer, boldly opaque, cool pink, warm bronze or deep berry. Some formulas feel creamy and others are more satin-like; the name alone won't tell you how much color or shine you'll get.

A gloss reflects light because of its shiny surface, while a frost gets its distinctive effect from the reflective particles in the color. You can wear them together, but you don't need both. A satin lipstick gives a softer sheen with less obvious pearl; a metallic formula may look more foil-like. Test a swatch in daylight and indoor light because shimmer can look very different in each.

## How to choose the effect you want

Start with the contrast you enjoy. A frost close to your natural lip color gives a subtle flash when you move. A pale silvery shade over a deeper base makes a more noticeable center highlight. A saturated red, plum or brown frost puts color and shine on equal footing.

If you're unsure about undertones, compare a rose or silver swatch with a peach or gold one in natural light. Notice which makes you feel most like yourself; your preferred jewelry or clothing can be a useful clue, but it doesn't restrict your options. A shade that looks striking rather than natural can be exactly the point.

A liner can also change the result. A pencil close to your lip color makes a soft boundary; a deeper brown, mauve or berry creates more definition. Use the side of a small brush or a clean fingertip to blur the inner edge so the transition feels deliberate.

## How to wear frost lipstick

- **Prep gently.** Smooth dry flakes with a damp washcloth if needed, then apply a little balm. Blot excess so lipstick can grip.

- **Line and blend.** Harper suggests a cool-toned liner several shades deeper than your natural lip color for a '90s effect, blending the meeting point with a small brush. Keep the outline visible if you like; soften the transition. Avoid these common [lip liner mistakes](https://makeup.allwomenstalk.com/lip-liner-mistakes-thatll-ruin-your-look/).

- **Add frost.** Apply from the center out, building the shine slowly. Dab with a fingertip for a softer finish.

- **Choose the rest of your makeup.** Keep cheeks soft to let the lip stand out, or add a smoky eye if you want full retro drama. MAC artist Kyle Anderson describes both approaches to Cosmopolitan.

- **Experiment with layers.** A sheer pearly shade over another lipstick can change its finish without requiring an entirely new color wardrobe.

### Three easy ways to apply it

**A soft everyday wash:** Apply balm, blot, and tap frost over the center of the lips. Press lips together, then add a touch more where you want the light to catch. The result is closer to a veil than a statement lip.

**A defined retro lip:** Outline with a deeper pencil, blend the line slightly inward, and fill the center with frost. Keep the outer edge clean with a cotton swab if you like. Harper's cool-toned liner technique is a useful starting point, but you can adjust the color and contrast to suit your taste.

**A metallic topper:** Apply your favorite lipstick first, blot, then dab a thin layer of frost in the center. Blend outward just enough to avoid a sharp patch. A warm brown base can soften an icy topper; a berry base can make a pink pearl look richer. Experiment with what you already own before buying another shade.

### A note on preparation

Frost can draw attention to texture, so work gently. A damp washcloth and balm are enough for many people; scrubbing hard or applying lipstick over irritated, cracked skin can make lips feel worse. If balm is very slippery, blot it before lining. You want a comfortable surface, not a thick layer that causes the liner to slide.

### How to test a shade before committing

Swatch on your hand to compare undertones, but remember that the natural color of your lips changes the final effect. If a hygienic tester is available, follow the retailer's instructions, or compare a swatch held near your face. Step into daylight if possible and check again under indoor lighting.

Look at the finish from more than one angle. A tube that looks muted straight on may flash silver when you turn. If the color seems too stark, try a deeper liner, a thinner layer or a familiar lipstick underneath. If it's too subtle, add another thin layer rather than piling on a thick coat at once.

## Frosty eyes, made wearable

Tap a pearly shadow at the inner corner or the center of the lid with a fingertip. Blend the edge into your usual neutral shadow, then add mascara. For a stronger look, sweep silver across the lid or pair a frosted lip with a smoky eye. Keep glitter and rhinestones away from the eye unless the product is intended for that area.

### Three eye looks to try

- **Inner-corner light:** Tap a small amount of pearly champagne or silver at the inner corner and blend the edges. Keep it on the surrounding skin and use only eye-safe products.

- **A soft lid wash:** Sweep a sheer taupe, rose or pale pewter shimmer across the mobile lid. Add a matte shade through the crease if you want definition, then mascara.

- **A smoky contrast:** Blend a deeper shadow along the lash line and outer corner, then press a little frost onto the center of the lid. The darker base gives the shimmer a frame without requiring a precise graphic shape.

For a playful editorial take, [Allure's coverage of frosted makeup](https://www.allure.com/story/bella-hadid-frosted-makeup) describes pearly and icy accents around the eyes and nose. Translate the idea into a single small highlight if that's more comfortable for you. Avoid craft glitter near eyes, and remove eye makeup gently at the end of the day.

## Pairing frost with the rest of your makeup

A pared-back complexion lets the lip finish stand out: use your usual base where you want it, brush brows into place and add a little mascara. Or embrace the nostalgia with a smoky eye and a sculpted liner. Neither is more correct; the difference is how much contrast you want overall.

For blush, a soft rose or peach can echo the lipstick without matching it exactly. If you're wearing a cool silver lip, a neutral cheek can keep the effect cohesive; if you choose bronze frost, a warmer cheek can repeat that warmth. These are styling suggestions, not color rules. Try your finished look in the light where you'll wear it.

## Keep the finish intentional

- Blend the edge between liner and lipstick instead of leaving a hard ring unless that's the look you want.

- Build thin layers so the frost doesn't settle heavily into texture.

- If a pale color looks gray against your preferred makeup, try a warmer or deeper base; you can also lean into the icy contrast.

- Try one feature first, then add more shine if you love it. There is no rule against a full frosted face.

## Common frost lipstick problems and fixes

**The finish looks patchy.** Remove the lipstick, apply a small amount of balm and blot, then build thin layers. A creamy lip pencil underneath can provide a more even base; test the combination because formulas can behave differently together.

**The color looks too pale.** Blend in a deeper liner or layer the frost over a lipstick you already like. You can also keep the pale shade concentrated at the center rather than covering the entire lip.

**The liner looks too harsh.** Use a small brush to pull a little liner inward before adding lipstick. Add color gradually until the edge has the level of definition you prefer. A visible outline can also be intentional if you enjoy it.

**The shine disappears quickly.** Blotting removes some reflective product, so add a light final layer after blotting the base color. Bring the lipstick for a touch-up rather than adding heavy layers that may feel uncomfortable.

**The shimmer emphasizes dryness.** Choose a creamier formula or wear the frost as a small topper over balm or lipstick. Skip exfoliation if your lips are already irritated and give them time to recover.

## Frequently asked questions

### What liner goes with frost lipstick?

A deeper cool-toned liner makes a classic contrast; a brown, berry or lip-toned pencil can also work. Blend where the two colors meet.

### Can deep skin wear pale frost?

Absolutely. Try it sheer, with a deeper liner or as a topper, then adjust until the contrast feels right to you.

### Is frost the same as gloss?

Frost has a pearly or metallic reflective finish. Gloss adds shine and may be clear or colored; a product can have both qualities. For a different shine, try [stained glass lips](https://makeup.allwomenstalk.com/stained-glass-lips/).

### Can I wear frosted lipstick during the day?

Yes. Tap on a sheer layer or use it as a center highlight for a subtle effect. A bold metallic lip also works in daylight if that's the look you want.

### Should lip liner be lighter or darker than the lipstick?

Either can work. A deeper liner creates contrast and definition, while a close match gives a softer edge. Blend and adjust until you like the transition.

### Can I put gloss over frost?

Yes. A small amount adds a wet shine over the pearl. Gloss may make the color move more, so apply sparingly and touch up as needed.

### What is the difference between pearl and glitter?

Pearl usually describes a finer reflective sheen; glitter tends to have more visibly distinct particles. Product names vary, so inspect the finish and check that a product is intended for the area where you plan to use it.
