# Highlighter Under Your Eyes? How the Trick Works and Who Should Skip It

A tiny amount of subtle highlighter can make the under-eye area look brighter, but it does not cover dark circles like concealer. The trick works best when you want a light-reflecting finish rather than much coverage. If shimmer emphasizes texture or a pale shade turns your circles gray, a corrector and concealer may suit you better.

## What is the under-eye highlighter trend?

It moves highlighter from the cheekbones to the area where you would normally apply concealer. The appeal is a lighter-looking finish with fewer layers. It is a makeup technique, not a treatment for dark circles, and a flattering result in one video does not establish how it will look in daylight on someone else.

Armani Beauty Australia's National Face Designer Jason Branigan tells [Nine.com.au](https://www.nine.com.au/lifestyle/fashion-beauty/highlighter-under-eyes-beauty-trend-how-it-works-makeup-artist-advice-20261027-p617hn.html) that highlighter brightens by reflecting light. His approach still uses a little concealer where coverage is needed. Makeup artist Ellen Malone, known as @AskEllenBeauty, is more skeptical: the wrong color or shimmer can emphasize exactly what a reader hoped to soften.

## Highlighter, concealer or corrector?

Product
Its job in the routine

Highlighter
Add luminosity; it does not provide the same coverage as concealer.

Concealer
Add targeted pigment coverage where you want it.

Color corrector
Adjust the appearance of a discoloration before concealer, using a suitable shade.

You can combine them, but you do not need to. More layers are not automatically a better solution.

## Who might like it—and who might skip it

Consider trying a small amount if you want a soft glow and do not need much coverage. It may also fit a minimal base routine where you want some darkness to remain visible rather than cover everything.

Adapt or skip it if:

- Your circles look grayer with a pale highlighter.

- You dislike the way shimmer draws attention to fine lines or texture.

- You want substantial coverage rather than a change in reflectivity.

- The product is not intended for the eye area.

Malone's caution is useful: a product can be pretty on the cheekbone and less flattering a few centimeters higher. Trying the trend is optional; keeping concealer is not failing it.

## How to try it in four steps

The formula and shade ideas below follow [Bustle's explainer](https://www.bustle.com/beauty/highlighter-under-eyes-brighten-dark-circles) and Branigan's advice in Nine.

- **Choose a subtle liquid or cream.** Avoid chunky glitter. Powder can emphasize dryness or creasing, according to Branigan.

- **Stay close to your skin tone.** Bustle suggests beige-champagne shades for fair skin and gold or copper for medium-to-deep complexions. Treat these as starting points; undertone and the individual formula still matter.

- **Tap on very little.** Blend a small amount on the inner under-eye skin outward toward the upper cheek. Keep it away from the lash line and the eye itself.

- **Add concealer only where you want coverage.** Blend the edges rather than automatically covering the whole luminous area. Our [concealer application guide](https://makeup.allwomenstalk.com/tips-for-applying-your-concealer-flawlessly/) has more everyday technique ideas.

Check your result in ordinary daylight as well as the bathroom mirror. If you prefer the side without highlighter, that is useful information—not a reason to keep adding more.

## A color-corrector alternative

Malone suggests peach for lighter blue-toned darkness and orange for deeper blue tones, followed by concealer or foundation. The correct depth matters: a vivid corrector that remains visible under your base is not the finish you were aiming for.

Start with a very small amount and adjust. Our [color-correcting guide](https://makeup.allwomenstalk.com/what-should-you-know-about-color-correcting-makeup/) explains the cosmetic principle. This is about makeup color; it does not identify the medical cause of under-eye darkness.

## Keep the eye area in mind

Use only a product intended for use around the eyes, following its label. Do not assume every face highlighter is eye-safe. Stop using it if it causes irritation. If irritation persists or you have eye symptoms, get professional advice rather than covering the area with more makeup.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you put highlighter under your eyes?

A suitable eye-area formula can add brightness. Whether you like the result depends on the shade, texture and coverage you want.

### Does it cover dark circles?

It changes how light catches the area; it does not replace concealer's coverage.

### What formula should I choose?

A subtle liquid or cream without chunky glitter is the approach described in the sources. Check the eye-area directions.

### Does concealer go over or under it?

In Branigan's version, a little concealer goes over the highlighter only where needed.

### Who should skip it?

Anyone who dislikes the extra shimmer, finds it emphasizes texture, gets a gray cast or cannot find a suitable eye-area formula can stick with concealer or corrector.
